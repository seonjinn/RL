This verifies matched short-run execution and comparable token volume. The 1.281x speedup is the measurement from this run; no run-to-run uncertainty was collected. The higher MXFP8 KL range also requires a longer convergence and accuracy study before making an accuracy claim. The SLURM job failed after trace save because Ray initialized a core worker twice during Python finalization; all rank artifacts had already passed the file gate.

What should change next?

  1. Keep tuning expert grouped BMM. MoE still owns 46.5% of MXFP8 active GPU time, and FC1 is the larger remaining family. Record the routed M-bucket frequency and tune the common buckets first. If the remaining MoE time were halved with all other work fixed, this profile bounds the additional throughput gain at about 1.28x.
  2. Tune or fuse routing/finalize. It rose 4.8% per token and does not benefit from lower-precision weights.
  3. Persist the autotune cache. Cold start profiled 21 MoE inputs on each worker for about 18 minutes. Warm all quantization shapes to remove the observed inference-time JIT warning.
  4. Then address attention. Its 21.3 s/Mtoken is unchanged and becomes the next large non-MoE floor.
Practical consequence
In this matched run, MoE-only MXFP8 raises steady throughput by 28.1%. The next patch should target grouped-BMM tactics and routing, while moving autotuning out of job startup.
